The trainable conductor of fall-os

Didy

One loop. Your models. Nothing leaves without your say-so.

A Didy is the conductor: the single control loop that turns a plain-language instruction into work across everything you own. It runs on your own models first and escalates to a paid API only where you have allowed it — and that is a structural guarantee, not a setting. A fresh Didy ships blank; you train it by registering organs and sign it with your own prefix.

What it is

A conductor you train, not an assistant you rent

Every request runs the same five phases, and each one calls the shared core rather than its own logic — so improving the core improves every organ the Didy conducts.

Blank on arrival

Ships untrained

A fresh instance is didy — no organs, no owner. It is a slot, not a product with someone else's decisions baked in.

You train it

Register organs

Registering an organ tells it how to expand options, how to score them, and optionally how to commit. Its own logic is untouched.

You sign it

It becomes yours

Sign it with your prefix and it becomes a <prefix>-didy — a named instance on the lineage, distinct from anyone else's.

Grounded

It never commits alone

It expands and scores, then waits. A candidate is selected only when an author commits — a person, or an automated check you configured.

Bounded

It cannot overspend

Autonomy is granted per organ and bounded by a signed budget it cannot exceed, because the grant is what it holds.

Remembers

Keeps what it passed over

Every alternative it did not take is indexed, so a direction you keep circling surfaces on its own instead of being lost.

The cascade

Local first. Frontier only if you allow it.

A request descends to the cheapest tier that can answer it. The first four run on hardware you own. The last two leave the machine, so they are consent-gated — and if you have not granted them, the provider is never invoked at all. The prompt is not serialised outward, so there is nothing to leak.
